## The three papers

Edexcel A Level Mathematics (9MA0) is assessed by three papers, all sat at the end of Year 13. There
is no coursework and no non-calculator paper — **a calculator is allowed in all three**.

| Paper | Content | Length | Marks | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper 1: Pure Mathematics 1** | Any pure topic from either year | 2 hours | 100 | 33⅓% |
| **Paper 2: Pure Mathematics 2** | Any pure topic from either year | 2 hours | 100 | 33⅓% |
| **Paper 3: Statistics and Mechanics** | Section A: Statistics (50 marks)<br>Section B: Mechanics (50 marks) | 2 hours | 100 | 33⅓% |

Two consequences that people underestimate:

- **Pure is two-thirds of the qualification.** If you are short on time, pure is where the marks are.
- **Papers 1 and 2 are not split by year or by topic.** Either paper can ask anything from the pure
  content. There is no "Paper 1 is Year 12". Everything is examinable everywhere.

The grade boundaries typically land somewhere around 55–65% for an A, and around 80% for an A*
(the A* is awarded on the overall total, not on a specific paper). Those move year to year — treat
them as a rough map, not a target.

## Timing

200 marks of pure in 240 minutes, so **1.2 minutes per mark**. A 6-mark question deserves about
7 minutes. If you're 3 minutes over on a question, leave it, mark it, come back. Students lose far
more marks to unfinished papers than to wrong answers.

## What's in Year 12 vs Year 13

The split below is the standard Edexcel teaching order. The specification itself doesn't formally
split AS from A2 content in the A Level, but this is how virtually every school sequences it, and
it's how this resource is organised.

### Pure — Year 12 (AS content)

Algebraic expressions · Quadratics · Equations and inequalities · Graphs and transformations ·
Straight-line graphs · Circles · Algebraic methods and proof · The binomial expansion ·
Trigonometric ratios · Trigonometric identities and equations · Vectors (2D) ·
Differentiation · Integration · Exponentials and logarithms

### Pure — Year 13 (A2 content)

Proof by contradiction and partial fractions · Functions and modulus · Sequences and series ·
Binomial expansion for negative and fractional indices · Radians · Reciprocal and inverse trig
functions · Addition formulae and the R-form · Parametric equations · Advanced differentiation ·
Numerical methods · Advanced integration and differential equations · Vectors (3D)

### Statistics

**Year 12:** Sampling · Measures of location and spread · Data representation · Correlation and
regression · Probability · The binomial distribution · Hypothesis testing (binomial)

**Year 13:** Conditional probability and set notation · The normal distribution · Normal
approximation to the binomial · Hypothesis testing with the normal distribution and with the PMCC ·
Non-linear regression

### Mechanics

**Year 12:** Modelling assumptions · Constant acceleration (SUVAT) · Forces and Newton's laws ·
Variable acceleration (calculus)

**Year 13:** Moments · Forces and friction on inclined planes · Projectiles · Applications of forces
(connected particles, equilibrium) · Further kinematics with vectors and calculus

## The large data set

Statistics questions can refer to the **Edexcel large data set** (weather data from five UK stations
and three overseas, for 1987 and 2015). You will not be given the data in the exam, but you are
expected to be familiar with it: the variable names, the units, what a realistic value looks like,
and the quirks (`tr` for trace rainfall, missing data, the fact that some stations report only some
variables).

The marks available are small but they are *free marks if you've seen the data and zero if you
haven't*. There's a dedicated guide in the Statistics section.

## Overarching themes

The specification names three themes that run through every paper. They're not separate topics —
they're the reason certain marks exist:

- **OT1 — Mathematical argument, language and proof.** "Show that", "prove", "hence deduce". Marks
  for logical structure and correct notation.
- **OT2 — Mathematical problem solving.** Multi-step questions where no method is named and you have
  to build the route yourself. This is where A/A* is decided.
- **OT3 — Mathematical modelling.** Translating a real situation into maths, stating assumptions,
  and criticising the model. Very common in mechanics and in exponential-growth questions.

:::insight Where the grades are actually decided
Almost everyone can do a standard "differentiate this" question. The grade separation happens on
questions that (a) combine two topics, (b) don't say which method to use, or (c) ask you to justify
or criticise rather than calculate. Those are the questions to practise deliberately — and they're
the ones this resource flags as "synoptic" throughout.
:::
