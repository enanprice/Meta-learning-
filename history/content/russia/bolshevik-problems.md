One essay plan for your teacher's fourth topic: **early issues faced by the Bolsheviks**. Dates before February 1918 are Old Style.

## Question: *"The Civil War was the greatest threat to Bolshevik power in the years 1917–21." How far do you agree?*

> **Line of argument:** agree, largely. The Civil War was the **greatest threat** because it was the one problem that could have physically destroyed the regime: White armies came within reach of Moscow and Petrograd in 1919. But the Bolsheviks faced other serious threats. **Political opposition** began with their minority vote in the Constituent Assembly. **War with Germany** forced the humiliating Brest-Litovsk treaty. **Economic collapse and popular revolt** peaked in 1921 with Tambov and Kronstadt. That last threat came after the Civil War was won, and it forced Lenin to retreat into NEP.

## Evidence bank

| Threat | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Political legitimacy | Constituent Assembly elections (November 1917): SRs 37.6% of the vote (324 of 767 seats), Bolsheviks 23.3% (183 seats). It met on 5 January 1918 and was dissolved the next day |
| Response | Kadets banned as "enemies of the people" (November 1917). The Cheka set up under Dzerzhinsky (December 1917) |
| The German war | Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918): lost lands holding 34% of the empire's population, 54% of its industrial land, 89% of its coalfields and 26% of its railways. A supplementary treaty (August 1918) added 6 billion marks in reparations. The Left SRs left the government |
| Civil War | Czech Legion revolt (May 1918). Whites: Kolchak (Siberia), Denikin (south), Yudenich (Petrograd). Foreign intervention: Britain, France, the USA and Japan |
| The 1919 peak | Denikin reached Orel, under 400 km from Moscow (October 1919). Yudenich reached the edge of Petrograd |
| Red Army | About 6.5 million men at the 1920 peak under Trotsky, using 48,000 former tsarist officers (by August 1920) watched by commissars |
| Terror | The royal family shot (July 1918). Red Terror declared (September 1918) after Fanny Kaplan shot Lenin |
| War Communism | Nationalisation (June 1918), grain requisitioning, private trade banned. Heavy industry output by 1921 was 20% of its 1913 level; Petrograd lost 70% of its population in 1918–20. Famine (1921–22): around 5 million dead |
| Revolt | Tambov peasant rising (1920–21). Kronstadt sailors' rising (March 1921), crushed |
| Retreat | NEP and the ban on factions, Tenth Party Congress (March 1921) |

- Civil War deaths, from fighting, terror, disease and famine, are usually estimated at 7–10 million.

## Introduction

- **Define:** a "threat to Bolshevik power" is anything that could have removed the Bolsheviks from government: military defeat, loss of legitimacy, or popular revolt.
- **Context:** in October 1917 the Bolsheviks controlled Petrograd and Moscow, but not most of Russia. They were a minority party at war with Germany, with a collapsing economy.
- **Judgement:** the Civil War was the greatest threat, but economic collapse and popular revolt in 1921 came close to matching it.

## Paragraph 1: The Civil War (the greatest threat)

- **Point:** the Civil War was the only threat that could have destroyed the regime outright.
- **Evidence:**
  - From May 1918 the Czech Legion and White armies controlled huge areas. Britain, France, the USA and Japan sent troops.
  - In 1919 Kolchak advanced from Siberia, Denikin reached Orel, under 400 km from Moscow, and Yudenich reached the edge of Petrograd.
  - The royal family was shot in July 1918 as the Czechs approached Ekaterinburg.
- **Analysis:** a White victory would have meant the end of Bolshevik rule. The Reds won through Trotsky's Red Army of about 6.5 million at its peak, their central position with the railways and industry, and White disunity.
- **Counter:** the Whites were divided, far apart and associated with the return of landlords, so they never coordinated their attacks. The foreign interventions were small.
- **Mini-judgement:** the greatest threat, because it was existential.

## Paragraph 2: Political opposition and the German war (serious, dealt with by force and concession)

- **Point:** the Bolsheviks lacked a democratic mandate and faced a war they could not win.
- **Evidence:**
  - The Constituent Assembly elections gave the SRs 37.6% to the Bolsheviks' 23.3%. Lenin dissolved the Assembly after one day.
  - Kadets were banned, the Cheka was created, and the Red Terror was declared in 1918.
  - Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) cost 34% of the population, 54% of industrial land and 89% of coalfields. The Left SRs quit the government in protest.
- **Analysis:** Lenin traded land for survival and used terror to remove political rivals, turning Russia into a one-party state.
- **Counter:** these decisions also fuelled the Civil War. Brest-Litovsk and the closing of the Assembly drove SRs and patriots into opposition. So this threat fed into the greatest one.
- **Mini-judgement:** serious, but solved by 1918 at the price of making the Civil War worse.

## Paragraph 3: Economic collapse and popular revolt (the threat of 1921)

- **Point:** by 1921 the Bolsheviks' own supporters were turning against them.
- **Evidence:**
  - War Communism's grain requisitioning provoked peasant resistance. Heavy industry output fell to 20% of 1913 levels by 1921, and Petrograd lost 70% of its population in 1918–20.
  - Famine in 1921–22 killed around 5 million.
  - The Tambov rising (1920–21) and the Kronstadt rising (March 1921), whose sailors had been heroes of 1917, demanded "soviets without Bolsheviks".
- **Analysis:** Lenin called Kronstadt "the flash that lit up reality". The threat was serious enough to force a major policy retreat: NEP and the ban on factions in March 1921.
- **Counter:** the revolts were local and crushed by the Red Army. They could not overthrow the regime the way the Whites might have.
- **Mini-judgement:** the most serious threat after the Civil War, and one that changed Bolshevik policy.

## Conclusion

- **Weigh it up:** the Civil War (P1) could have ended Bolshevik rule. Political opposition and Brest-Litovsk (P2) were overcome by force and concession in 1918. Economic collapse and revolt (P3) forced the 1921 retreat.
- **Final judgement:** the Civil War was the greatest threat because it was the only one that could have removed the Bolsheviks completely. But the crisis of 1921 shows that winning the war nearly lost them the people, and keeping power required both terror and economic retreat.

## Historians to mention

- **Richard Pipes:** the Bolsheviks survived through ruthless terror and dictatorship from the start.
- **Orlando Figes:** many Russians, especially peasants, sided with the Reds against the Whites as the "lesser evil", because the Whites meant the return of the landlords.
