One essay plan for your teacher's first topic: **tsarism and why it remained in control**. Dates before February 1918 are Old Style (Russia's calendar at the time).

## Question: *How far was repression the main reason for the survival of tsarism in the years 1894–1914?*

> **Line of argument:** repression was the **most important single reason**, because the regime survived its one real crisis, 1905, by force once the army stayed loyal. But repression alone was not enough. Tsarism also survived because it **made concessions at the right moment** (the October Manifesto split the opposition), because the **opposition was divided and weak**, and because many Russians stayed **loyal to the Tsar, the Church and tradition**. Repression worked best when combined with these.

## Evidence bank

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Secret police | The Okhrana (founded 1881) infiltrated revolutionary groups. Its agents were inside the SRs and Bolsheviks. Censorship, exile to Siberia |
| 1905: army loyalty | Troops fired on Bloody Sunday (9 January 1905). The Moscow rising was crushed in December 1905, with 1,059 rebels killed. Punitive expeditions along the Trans-Siberian Railway |
| Stolypin's repression | Field courts-martial (1906–07) executed over 1,000 people. The noose became known as "Stolypin's necktie" |
| Concessions | October Manifesto (17 October 1905): an elected Duma and civil rights. Redemption payments halved in 1906 and abolished from 1907 |
| Taking concessions back | Fundamental Laws (April 1906): the Tsar kept "supreme autocratic power" and could rule by decree (Article 87). The First Duma lasted 73 days, the Second about three months. The June 1907 electoral law favoured landowners |
| A divided opposition | SDs (1898) split into Bolsheviks and Mensheviks (1903). SRs (1901). Liberals split in 1905: Octobrists accepted the Manifesto, Kadets wanted more. Many revolutionary leaders, Lenin included, were in exile |
| Loyalty | Peasant faith in the Tsar as "little father". The Orthodox Church preached obedience. The Romanov tercentenary (1913) drew loyal crowds |
| Economic growth | Industrial growth about 8% a year in the 1890s and about 6% in 1908–13. Record harvest in 1913 |
| Warning signs | Lena Goldfields massacre (1912). Strikers: about 725,000 (1912), 887,000 (1913), 1.3 million in January–July 1914 |

## Introduction

- **Define:** "repression" means the use of force and control: the army, the Okhrana, censorship, exile and executions. "Survival" means the autocracy kept power, above all through the crisis of 1905.
- **Context:** Nicholas II (Tsar from 1894) believed in autocracy and rejected reform as "senseless dreams" (1895). He faced a growing working class, land-hungry peasants and new revolutionary parties.
- **Judgement:** repression was the main reason, but it worked because it came with well-timed concessions and against a divided opposition.

## Paragraph 1: Repression (the main reason)

- **Point:** when the regime was seriously threatened, force saved it.
- **Evidence:**
  - The Okhrana infiltrated and disrupted revolutionary groups. Leaders were exiled or forced abroad.
  - In 1905 the army stayed loyal once the war with Japan ended. It crushed the Moscow rising (December 1905) and arrested the St Petersburg Soviet.
  - Stolypin's field courts-martial executed over 1,000 people in 1906–07 and ended peasant unrest.
- **Analysis:** 1905 shows the key point. The regime survived because the army obeyed. In February 1917 the army mutinied and tsarism fell, which proves how much depended on the soldiers.
- **Counter:** repression also created martyrs and anger (Bloody Sunday destroyed the image of the "little father"). On its own it did not end the 1905 revolution; the October Manifesto did that first.
- **Mini-judgement:** the most important factor, but not a sufficient one.

## Paragraph 2: Concessions, then taking them back (crucial in 1905)

- **Point:** the regime gave just enough to split its opponents, then clawed most of it back.
- **Evidence:**
  - The October Manifesto (1905) satisfied the liberals. The Octobrists took their name from it. Witte also negotiated peace with Japan, freeing the army.
  - Redemption payments were abolished from 1907, and Stolypin's land reforms aimed to create loyal peasant farmers.
  - The Fundamental Laws (1906) kept the Tsar's "supreme autocratic power". The first two Dumas were dissolved, and the 1907 electoral law produced a cooperative Third Duma.
- **Analysis:** concessions bought time and divided the opposition at the moment of greatest danger. That made repression possible.
- **Counter:** clawing back the concessions left liberals disillusioned. That came back to haunt the Tsar in 1915–17.
- **Mini-judgement:** the essential partner of repression in 1905.

## Paragraph 3: A weak opposition and continuing loyalty (supporting reasons)

- **Point:** tsarism survived partly because its enemies were weak and many Russians stayed loyal.
- **Evidence:**
  - The opposition was split: Bolsheviks against Mensheviks, SRs against SDs, liberals against socialists. Their leaders were in exile, and the Okhrana had agents inside.
  - Most peasants still revered the Tsar, and the Church backed the autocracy. The 1913 tercentenary celebrations showed real public loyalty.
  - Economic growth after 1908 brought some prosperity, and the 1913 harvest was a record.
- **Analysis:** without a united opposition, there was no alternative government to take power, even in 1905.
- **Counter:** the strike wave of 1912–14 suggests support was weakening before the war.
- **Mini-judgement:** important background conditions that made repression effective.

## Conclusion

- **Weigh it up:** repression (P1) saved the regime in 1905 because the army stayed loyal. Concessions (P2) split the opposition first. Weak opposition and loyalty (P3) meant there was no alternative.
- **Final judgement:** repression was the main reason tsarism survived to 1914, but only in combination with concessions and a divided opposition. 1917 is the test: when the army deserted the Tsar, nothing else could save him.

## Historians to mention

- **Optimists** (for example, those who stress Stolypin's reforms and 1908–13 growth): tsarism was recovering and could have survived without the war.
- **Pessimists** (for example, Orlando Figes): the regime's refusal to share power made collapse likely; the war only sped it up.
