One essay plan for your teacher's third topic: **the events of February–October 1917 for the Provisional Government (PG)**. Dates are Old Style. October 25 is 7 November by the Western calendar.

## Question: *How far was the decision to continue the war the main reason for the Provisional Government's loss of power in October 1917?*

> **Line of argument:** continuing the war was the **main reason**, because it caused or worsened every other failure: the economic crisis, army collapse, the June Offensive, the Kornilov Affair, and the failure to deal with land. But the PG was also **weak by its nature** (unelected, temporary and sharing power with the Soviet), and the **Bolsheviks under Lenin and Trotsky** exploited its failures. The war explains why the PG lost support. Bolshevik organisation explains why it lost power to Lenin's party.

## Evidence bank

| Date | Event |

| 27 Feb–2 Mar | Petrograd Soviet formed (27 February). Provisional Government formed under Prince Lvov (2 March). Kerensky the only socialist minister |
| 1 Mar | Soviet Order No. 1: soldiers to obey the Soviet first; committees to control weapons |
| March | PG reforms: political amnesty, freedom of speech and assembly, end of religious and national discrimination, death penalty abolished. Constituent Assembly promised |
| 3–4 Apr | Lenin returns; April Theses: no support for the PG, "All power to the Soviets", "Peace, Bread, Land" |
| 18 Apr–May | The Milyukov Note promises to fight on, sparking the April Crisis. Milyukov and Guchkov resign; first coalition with socialists |
| 18 Jun | Kerensky's June Offensive begins and collapses within weeks, with heavy losses and mass desertion |
| 3–7 Jul | July Days: armed demonstrations in Petrograd. Bolsheviks blamed; Lenin called a German agent and flees to Finland; Trotsky arrested |
| Jul | Kerensky becomes Prime Minister. Death penalty restored at the front |
| 27–30 Aug | Kornilov Affair: Kerensky arms the Red Guards and releases Bolsheviks to defend Petrograd |

- Throughout 1917 peasants seized land without waiting for the PG. Inflation and food shortages continued, and desertion from the army soared.
- Bolshevik membership rose from 24,000 in February 1917 to 200,000 by September 1917.

## Introduction

- **Define:** "loss of power" means both the PG's loss of support during 1917 and its overthrow by the Bolsheviks on 25 October.
- **Context:** the PG was a committee of Duma politicians who took power after the Tsar abdicated. It saw itself as temporary until a Constituent Assembly could be elected.
- **Judgement:** continuing the war was the main reason the PG lost support, but its weak position and the Bolsheviks' leadership explain how it lost power.

## Paragraph 1: Continuing the war (the main reason)

- **Point:** the PG's decision to keep fighting caused its most damaging crises.
- **Evidence:**
  - The Milyukov Note (April) caused the April Crisis and forced Milyukov out.
  - The June Offensive collapsed, with heavy losses, and desertion soared.
  - The war kept inflation, food shortages and transport breakdown going.
  - It also delayed land reform and elections. The PG argued it couldn't hold elections or redistribute land while soldiers were at the front.
- **Analysis:** the war tied the PG to the Allies and the old elites, and left it unable to offer what people wanted: peace, bread and land. The Bolsheviks offered exactly that.
- **Counter:** leaving the war was very hard: the Allies' loans, national honour, and fear of German occupation. Lenin's own peace at Brest-Litovsk later cost 34% of the population.
- **Mini-judgement:** the main reason the PG lost support.

## Paragraph 2: The PG's weak position: Dual Power (structural weakness)

- **Point:** the PG lacked the authority to govern, whatever its policies.
- **Evidence:**
  - It was unelected, made up of Duma politicians from the restricted 1912 electorate.
  - Order No. 1 meant soldiers obeyed the Soviet first, so the PG did not control the army.
  - It saw itself as temporary, so it postponed big decisions (land, elections) for the Constituent Assembly.
  - Its freedoms let opponents organise freely, including the Bolsheviks.
- **Analysis:** Dual Power meant the PG had responsibility without power, so every crisis weakened it.
- **Counter:** the PG did have real support at first, and its reforms were popular. It was its decisions that lost it support.
- **Mini-judgement:** a serious weakness that made the war's effects fatal.

## Paragraph 3: Kornilov and the Bolsheviks (how it lost power)

- **Point:** the Bolsheviks, led by Lenin and Trotsky, turned the PG's failures into a seizure of power, and the Kornilov Affair gave them their chance.
- **Evidence:**
  - Lenin's April Theses gave the Bolsheviks clear, popular slogans: "Peace, Bread, Land" and "All power to the Soviets".
  - The July Days set them back, but the Kornilov Affair (August) let them pose as defenders of the revolution. Kerensky armed the Red Guards.
  - By September they had majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets. Trotsky's Military Revolutionary Committee organised the rising of 24–25 October, which met little resistance.
- **Analysis:** without Lenin's insistence (the 10–2 vote) there would probably have been no October rising. But the Bolsheviks succeeded because almost no one would defend the PG.
- **Counter:** the Bolshevik seizure was a coup by a minority. Even the Constituent Assembly elections in November gave them only about a quarter of the vote.
- **Mini-judgement:** this explains the timing and the winner, but not why the PG was so weak.

## Conclusion

- **Weigh it up:** the war (P1) drained the PG's support. Dual Power (P2) left it unable to act. The Bolsheviks (P3) took advantage, helped by Kornilov.
- **Final judgement:** continuing the war was the main reason the PG lost power. It made every other problem worse and handed the Bolsheviks their best slogan, "Peace". But the PG fell to the Bolsheviks specifically because of their leadership and organisation, and because the Kornilov Affair armed them.

## Historians to mention

- **Soviet view:** October was a popular revolution of workers and soldiers.
- **Liberal or Western view (for example, Richard Pipes):** October was a coup by a small, determined party.
- **Revisionist view (for example, Sheila Fitzpatrick):** the Bolsheviks rode a genuine wave of popular radicalism in 1917.
